CASE STUDY / 01
Product Design — Small Business Tools

Designing a Simple Customer & Billing Portal for a Small Print Business

From understanding the real business workflow → identifying the MVP → designing → building → validating → refining.

Role
Product Designer
01

Introduction

A small print business managing customer information and payments manually.

Victory Printers is a small printing business where customer orders involve multiple steps — from collecting requirements to designing, reviewing, printing, and payment.

The business didn't need a complex management system. They needed a simple way to keep customer information and bills organized and accessible.

02

Understanding the Business

Before designing anything, I spoke with the people actually running the process.

USER INTERVIEWS
Jeyaseelan
Business Owner / Customer Handling
Karthika
Graphic Designer / Customer & Account Handling
WHAT I WANTED TO UNDERSTAND
  • How does a customer move through the business?
  • What information is currently recorded?
  • Where is customer/payment information stored?
  • What do they actually need?
  • What information is unnecessary for an MVP?
03

Existing Business Workflow

Customer arrives
↓
Jeyaseelan discusses requirements
↓
Certificates / invitations / printing requirements
↓
Karthika creates the design
↓
Customer reviews design → design finalized
↓
Printing
↓
Order handed to customer
↓
Payment recorded manually
KEY OBSERVATION The problem was how customer and billing information was being managed around that process.
04

What I Learned From Users

Jeyaseelan
Business Owner
"I mainly need to quickly find customers and see their bills."
NEEDS
  • Customer information
  • Customer bills
  • Easy access
Karthika
Graphic Designer
"I need access to the same customer information."
NEEDS
  • Same customer data
  • Same billing information
  • No separate system
IMPORTANT INSIGHT Both users wanted the same source of truth. They didn't ask for complex order management, employee management, advanced analytics, detailed workflow tracking, or multiple account systems.
05

User Needs → Product Requirements

After the interviews, I translated the conversations into product requirements.

User NeedProduct Requirement
Find customers quicklyCustomer list + search
View customer informationCustomer profile
Track billsCustomer invoices
Add new customersAdd customer
Record a billCreate invoice
Both users need same dataShared data
Keep the product simpleAvoid unnecessary features
THE KEY PRINCIPLE

Build only what the users actually need.

06

MVP

CUSTOMERS
  • View customers
  • Search customers
  • Add customer
  • Edit customer
BILLING
  • Create invoice
  • View invoices
  • Track payment received
07

Product Goal

USER GOAL

Find and manage customer information and bills without relying on manual records.

BUSINESS GOAL

Create a simple shared system that makes customer and billing information easier to manage.

PRODUCT GOAL

Provide one lightweight source of truth for customers and their bills.

08

Designing the Experience

Dashboard
→
Customers
→
Search / Select Customer
→
Customer Details
→
View Bills
→
Add / Edit Customer or Invoice

The design intentionally avoids unnecessary navigation and features.

09

User Feedback

THEY DIDN'T NEED
  • Partial payment status
  • Detailed pending-payment breakdowns
  • Additional KPIs
  • Extra operational information
THEY WANTED
  • Customers
  • Customer details
  • Bills
  • Payment information
  • Simple access to the data
TAKEAWAY

More information doesn't automatically mean more value.

10

Final Product

1. CUSTOMERS
  • Add customers
  • Search customers
  • View customer information
  • Edit customer information
2. BILLS
  • Create bills
  • View bills
  • See payment information
RESULT A lightweight portal that matches the actual business requirement instead of trying to become a full business-management system.

12

Impact

Replacing manual records with one shared, focused source of truth.

FOR THE OWNER

Jeyaseelan can find a customer and their bill status in seconds instead of digging through paper records.

FOR THE TEAM

Jeyaseelan and Karthika now work from the same customer and billing data, removing the need to relay information manually.

FOR THE BUSINESS

A lightweight system that fits how Victory Printers actually operates, with room to grow only if the business needs it.

13

What I Learned

01

Start with the business, not the interface.

I first understood how Victory Printers actually operates.

02

User interviews can change the scope.

The initial concept had more information than the users needed.

03

MVP means saying no.

Removing features was as important as adding them.

04

Real users expose assumptions.

The feedback helped me remove partial-payment statuses, KPIs, and other unnecessary elements.
